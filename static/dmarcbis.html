Technical Briefing

The Architecture of DMARCbis

Moving email authentication from volunteer-run text files to native DNS infrastructure.

For over a decade, DMARC (RFC 7489) has served as the global floor for email authentication. It allows domain owners to declare: "If it didn't come from my approved list, don't deliver it." However, the original specification rested on a structural paradox. To function, it required an answer to a deceptively simple question: Where does your organizational domain end?

To answer that, every major email receiver in the world has been relying on a text file hosted on GitHub.

The Era of the Public Suffix List (PSL)

Since 2007, the Public Suffix List (a Mozilla Foundation project) has cataloged every public domain suffix on the internet. From standard TLDs like .com to private suffixes like github.io, the PSL told the receiver where the "public" part ended and the "organizational" part began.

Why the dependency had to change:

While the PSL volunteers are internet heroes, the structural risks were real: the IETF didn't own the list, manual updates created latency for new TLDs, and a static text file is a fragile way to manage a protocol operating at global scale.

The DMARCbis Solution: The DNS Tree Walk

DMARCbis transitions DMARC to a formal Standards Track RFC and introduces the DNS Tree Walk. Instead of consulting a downloaded file, the receiver queries the DNS hierarchy directly.

  • The Source: The receiver queries for a DMARC record at the exact domain found in the From: header.
  • The Upward Walk: If no record exists, the receiver "walks" up the tree, stripping the leftmost label and querying the next level.
  • The Termination: The walk continues until a record is found or it hits a "Public Suffix" identified via native DNS signals. This typically terminates within eight levels.

Technical Hygiene: Your Transition Checklist

DMARCbis is an operational "cleanup." It incorporates a decade of production data to remove legacy friction and harden the standard.

  • Prune Legacy Tags: The pct=, rf=, and ri= tags are being retired. DMARC is now binary; the policy applies to 100% of mail or 0%.
  • Close the Subdomain Gap: Use the np= (No Policy) tag to explicitly set a policy for non-existent subdomains, protecting the "dark corners" of your domain space.
  • Explicit Identity: Use psd=n to tell receivers you are not a public registry, ensuring the Tree Walk algorithm terminates correctly.
  • Stricter Parsing: Ensure rua= and ruf= tags begin with the mailto: schema. Bare email addresses will likely fail in stricter parsers.
  • External Reporting: Verification records at [your-domain]._report._dmarc.[destination-domain] are now essential for third-party report delivery.
