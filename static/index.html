DMARC is the policy layer that ties SPF and DKIM together. Understanding how it works, and what changed in the latest standard, is essential for interpreting your audit results.

What DMARC Does

DMARC lets a domain owner publish a policy telling receivers what to do when a message fails both SPF and DKIM alignment. A message passes DMARC if at least one of SPF or DKIM passes and the authenticated domain aligns with the From address. DKIM alignment is more resilient because DKIM signatures survive mail forwarding, while SPF breaks when a message is relayed through an intermediary.

RFC 9716 Replaces RFC 7489

RFC 9716 was published in November 2024 as a Proposed Standard, officially superseding RFC 7489 (March 2015). This app checks against RFC 9716. Key changes:

Tree Walk Replaces Public Suffix List
RFC 7489 used the Public Suffix List (PSL) to find the Organizational Domain, then checked for a DMARC record there. RFC 9716 replaces this with a tree walk: receivers walk up the DNS hierarchy level by level until they find a DMARC record. The tree walk is more accurate and doesn't depend on an external list.
pct Tag Removed
The pct= tag (which applied policy to only a percentage of failing messages) is removed with no replacement. The intended deployment path is: use p=none for monitoring, then move directly to enforcement.
ri and rf Tags Removed
The ri= (report interval) and rf= (report format) tags are removed. Receivers always sent reports on their own schedule and only used one format (AFRF), making both tags effectively unused.
New Tags: np, t, psd
np= sets policy for non-existent subdomains. t=y enables test mode, which drops the effective policy one level (reject becomes quarantine, quarantine becomes none) for cautious deployment. psd= signals whether a domain is a Public Suffix Domain.

Adoption Status

RFC 9716 is the authoritative standard, but most receivers (Gmail, Outlook, Yahoo) still implement RFC 7489 behavior as of early 2025. This means tree walk inheritance, the np= tag, and the t= tag may not be honored by all receivers yet. Where this matters, the audit results include a note about receiver adoption.
